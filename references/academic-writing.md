# Academic writing guidance

The user's requirements and voice come first; these are defaults.

- Formal, precise, impersonal-where-conventional language; keep source terminology exactly where accuracy depends on it.
- One main point per paragraph; claim → evidence → interpretation. Avoid filler, repetition and vague intensifiers.
- Separate and label: **observed evidence** (what a source/measurement shows), **source-derived claims** (cited), **interpretation** (yours, hedged appropriately), **recommendations**.
- Every factual claim needs a citation to an inventory source or an explicit marker `[[EVIDENCE NEEDED: …]]`. No unsupported statistics, no invented results.
- Define abbreviations at first use. Refer to every figure/table in the text before it appears.
- Abstract: purpose, method, main findings (only if they exist in the evidence), conclusion; no citations unless required.
- Follow assignment-specific structure and limits exactly; word counts exclude front matter/code per `validate_markdown.py` — confirm with the user how the assignment counts.
- Conflicting sources: present both with citations and flag for the user; never pick silently.
- When rewriting on request, change only what was asked and report what changed.
